Green Open Access Across India

A practical introduction to renewable power procurement for commercial and industrial consumers.

Open-access mechanisms can connect eligible consumers with renewable generators, but thresholds, charges, banking, metering and application processes depend on the state, consumer category and procurement route. The examples below are specifically for Andhra Pradesh and must not be applied as an all-India tariff schedule.

Banking and Settlement

Banking rules, settlement periods, permitted adjustments and charges vary across states and can change. Confirm the current state order, contract terms and energy-accounting process before relying on banked units in a financial model.

Official References

  1. Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy Through Green Energy Open Access) Rules, 2022, G.S.R. 418(E) — national framework; check later amendments before relying on a provision.
  2. Electricity Act, 2003 — India Code — central legislation concerning open access and electricity regulation.
  3. APERC Regulations and Orders — Andhra Pradesh only. The index lists its Green Energy Open Access Charges and Banking Regulation 2024 and subsequent amendments, including Regulation 02 of 2026.

For other states, consult the relevant SERC, transmission utility, SLDC and DISCOM for current orders and schedules. References checked 6 October 2026; rules and orders may change.

Types of Green Open Access

1. Third-Party Open Access
A consumer contracts to buy power from a generator. Charges, eligibility and approvals depend on the state and route.
2. Group Captive
Consumers participate in ownership and meet applicable consumption tests. Structure, obligations and charge treatment require legal and regulatory review.
3. Captive Generation
A consumer or qualifying captive group owns and uses generation under applicable rules. Suitability depends on scale, ownership and consumption requirements.
