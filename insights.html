How to Calculate Landed Cost of Open Access in AP

Start with the generator tariff, then account for applicable network charges, surcharges, losses and banking effects. A quoted saving is meaningful only when compared against the same meter, period and usage.

5 min read • By P. ChennaKrishna Reddy
Read full insight →

Working formula: landed cost per delivered unit is the generator energy charge plus every applicable per-unit network or regulatory charge, adjusted for energy losses and any banking or scheduling costs in the agreement.

The site's earlier illustration used a generator tariff of ₹3.8–4.2/unit, CSS of ₹0.8–1.6, wheeling of ₹0.25–0.55, transmission of ₹0.2–0.35 and a 6% loss assumption. Those ranges do not all apply identically to every consumer, and a loss percentage is an energy adjustment rather than automatically a rupee charge.

The ₹5.2–6.0 landed-cost and ₹7.8–8.5 DISCOM figures are indicative examples, not a current tariff quotation. Compare the same billing period and include demand charges, taxes, contracted energy, time-of-day profile and any applicable exemptions before estimating savings.

Charge schedules change. Confirm current APERC, APTRANSCO and DISCOM schedules and the generator PPA before making a decision.

GROUP CAPTIVE • 26% RULE

Group Captive 26% Equity and 51% Consumption

The short version: qualifying captive users must satisfy both ownership and consumption tests. A group structure can pool several businesses, but each participant's share and use must be planned carefully.

4 min read • By P. ChennaKrishna Reddy
Read full insight →

In a simplified group-captive example, captive users collectively hold at least 26% ownership in the generating entity and consume at least 51% of the plant's annual generation, subject to the applicable legal tests and proportionality requirements.

If four participants split ownership equally, 26% divided by four is 6.5% of the total equity each. Equal equity is only a teaching example: actual shareholding and consumption allocations need to match the project's structure and applicable rules.

A special charge treatment may be available only while the group continues to meet the required tests. Ownership records, annual generation and consumption data, contracts, metering and compliance reporting all matter; a label in a PPA alone does not establish eligibility.

This is a plain-language overview, not legal advice. Have qualified counsel and the project team confirm the current Electricity Rules, amendments and project documents.

How a Wind-Solar Hybrid Could Serve a Cement Plant

Assumptions: 720,000 units used in a month; an example DISCOM bill of ₹57.6 lakh; and an example hybrid-supply bill of ₹40 lakh. Dividing by the units gives ₹8.00/unit and about ₹5.56/unit respectively, a difference of roughly ₹2.44/unit or ₹17.6 lakh for that month.

BANKING

Banking in AP: How Night Adjustment Works

When renewable generation and factory demand do not coincide, permitted surplus may be banked and adjusted later, subject to settlement rules and charges.

Read full insight →

In a simple energy example, if 100 units are eligible for banking and an 8% energy charge applies, 92 units remain for later adjustment. This arithmetic is only an illustration; the actual treatment depends on the current regulation, the consumer category and the settlement statement.

Banking does not mean unlimited storage. Check banking period, time blocks, carry-forward or lapse rules, permitted energy limits and settlement frequency. Match the generator's hourly output against the factory's load before sizing the contract.

Request the applicable schedule and a sample monthly energy-account statement. Verify the banked units, adjustment, charges and any unutilized balance with the relevant DISCOM and project operator.

ELIGIBILITY

My Factory Uses 150 kW: Can I Get Open Access?

A 150 kW demand is above the 100 kW threshold cited in the current site overview, but capacity alone does not establish eligibility or technical feasibility.

Read full insight →

Start by confirming the sanctioned load and connection category on the latest bill. Then check the current consumer threshold, voltage and metering requirements for the chosen route, along with the consumer's DISCOM area and any outstanding conditions.

For a preliminary review, collect 12 months of bills, sanctioned-load details, a single-line diagram, meter information and interval or time-of-day data if available. The generator and network operator must also confirm capacity and evacuation feasibility.

Smaller sites may explore aggregation or a group-captive structure, but that adds ownership, consumption, documentation and settlement obligations. A site assessment is needed before treating it as a viable option.

Rules and thresholds may change; confirm current requirements with APERC, the DISCOM and APTRANSCO before applying.

APTRANSCO • PROCESS

Open Access NOC: How Long Can It Take?

A three-week estimate can help with planning, but document readiness, DISCOM feasibility and network studies can change the actual schedule.

Read full insight →

The earlier planning example allocated about 3 days for application preparation, 10 days for a feasibility response and 15–20 days for the wider NOC process. These are working estimates, not official service guarantees or statutory deadlines.

Incomplete bills, metering details, single-line diagrams, generator documents or corrections can add time. Technical constraints, application volume and coordination between the DISCOM, APTRANSCO and generator also affect progress.

Build a tracker for submission date, acknowledgement, clarification requests, feasibility status and NOC status. Ask the relevant office or authorized project partner for current service timelines before committing to a start date.

The timeline varies by case and should not be used as a guaranteed commissioning date.
